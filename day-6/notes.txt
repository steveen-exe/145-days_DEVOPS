> dns lookup
    nslookup <>
    dig <>
    Clients request data; servers process and respond via protocols (HTTP, WebSockets)
    .DNS Lookup: Tools like dig and nslookup translate domain names (google.com) into IP addresses via
    your local resolver (127.0.0.53).
    TTL (Time to Live): Google sets a 3-second expiration timer; your
    local cache saves the IPs but deletes them the moment the countdown hits zero.
    On-Demand Requests: 
    Your computer never auto-refreshes expired records in the background; it only fetches fresh IPs when
    you actively click, browse, or run a command.
    Load Balancing: Because your cache constantly expires
    and forces fresh requests, Google can continuously shuffle or swap IPs to distribute heavy traffic
    and bypass broken servers.

> traceroute - traceroute maps the exact path data takes across the internet to reach a destination
| Hop Range | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Hops 1 – 3 | Private corporate network infrastructure (WSL Gateway, Office Router, Core Switch) |
| Hops 4 – 5 | ISP / Telecom provider routing (Convergent India) |
| Hops 6 – 9 | Google global network and data center infrastructure |
| Hops 7 – 8 | Load-balanced Google backbone routes for traffic optimization |
| Final Destination | Google Delhi Server (`del11s20-in-f14.1e100.net`) |
| Average End-to-End Latency | ~16–18 ms |
| Network Health | Excellent – low latency, no visible packet loss, stable routing path |

TTL used -> to prevent data packets from wandering around the internet forever if they get lost.
